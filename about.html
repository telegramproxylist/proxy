About

A plain-language reference for Telegram's proxy options

Telegram quietly ships two different proxy systems, and most explanations either assume you already know networking or skip the parts that actually matter for safety. This guide tries not to.

What this site is

Telegram Proxy Guide is a small, focused reference covering how SOCKS5 and MTProto proxies work with Telegram, how to configure each one, and what trade-offs come with using either. It's written for people who want to understand what they're connecting to before they connect to it — not just a list of servers to paste into a settings screen.

What it isn't

This isn't an official Telegram property, and it doesn't operate any proxy servers itself. It also isn't a place to find live, currently-online proxy addresses — those change constantly, and publishing a static list here would be out of date within hours. For an actively maintained list of SOCKS5 and MTProto proxies, the companion site is telegramproxies.com.

Why the split? Keeping the explanations here and the live server list on a dedicated site means the concepts stay stable and citable, while the list itself can be refreshed as often as it needs to be without cluttering a guide page.

How to use a proxy safely

  • Prefer a proxy source that's actively maintained and tests servers before listing them.
  • Remember that a proxy operator can see connection metadata — who you're connecting to and when — even when message content stays encrypted between you and Telegram's servers.
  • Don't reuse the same third-party proxy for anything you wouldn't want linked to your Telegram account.
  • If a proxy stops responding, remove it rather than leaving it configured — a dead proxy just means Telegram falls back to a direct connection.

Get in touch
